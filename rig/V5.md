## Phase 0: guard rails

- **Snapshot corpus (L17).** `python -m studio.snapshots --check` builds 379 items (313 one-mascot stickers covering
  every motion, prop, effect and worn item, every mascot, captions and colours; the app's 23 clips on Biscuit and 3 on
  each other mascot; the 4 small effects) and compares a hash of each with the v4 output. Recorded from the v4 code
  (ea22b21) in `studio/tests/snapshots.json`. All 379 are unchanged after phase 1.
- **A second mascot is no longer dropped silently (P34).** "a cat hugging a dog" still draws the dog, but the parse
  result marks the cat as left out, carries a note ("one mascot fits in a sticker for now, so it's the dog; the cat is
  left out"), counts the cat as not understood, and the editor strikes the word through and shows the note. "two
  foxes" says there's one fox. The spec is unchanged, so the 941 golden prompts stay right.
- **Turnaround sheets.** `python3 tools/turnaround.py` draws every mascot's rest pose with the guide lines new views
  must keep (crown, eye line, shoulders, ground) and their heights in canvas px; `tools/turnaround.py biscuit` gives
  one cell per view (front, 20 degrees, three-quarter, side, back), with an onion skin where a view isn't drawn yet.

## Phase 1: the scene layer

`mascot/scene.py` bakes one or two mascots into one Lottie document:

```python
from mascot.scene import Scene, Stage
sc = Scene("comfort", frames=120)
sc.add("a", cat, clip_a, Stage(x=-58, y=-4, scale=0.74))       # each mascot: its own Character and clip
sc.add("b", bunny, clip_b, Stage(x=64, y=10, scale=0.74))      # y > 0 is nearer the viewer, so in front
sc.pin("a", "R", 26, 88, ("b", "head_side", "L"), hand=118, bend=-1, blend=8)   # the cat's paw on the bunny's head
sc.lift("a", "right arm", over="b", t0=18, t1=96)             # drawn over the bunny only around the pat
doc = sc.build()
```

- Layer names are prefixed per mascot (`a/neck`, `b/neck`); each mascot hangs from its own `a/stage` null.
- Layers are ordered by the depth bands of `Character.DEPTH`: effects over the head for every mascot, then each mascot
  from the front back (with what is lifted over it), then effects behind the mascots, then the shadows.
- `Scene.point(id, part, frame, side)` gives a named part in scene coordinates: crown, top, nose, mouth, chin, eye,
  cheek, ear, head_side, collar, chest, belly, back, shoulder, hip, paw, wrist, foot, ground.
- `Scene.pin(...)` solves a paw towards a part of the other mascot with the existing IK, after every mascot is
  prepared, so the target moves with the partner. `Scene.reach(pin)` says how far the arm must stretch (the IK caps at
  1.7 times its rest length; aim for 1.5 or less).
- A scene of one mascot on a plain stage writes exactly the file `Character.build()` writes. `Character.build()`
  itself now runs in three stages (`prepare`, `finish`, `bake`) so a scene can solve its pins between them.

`studio/scene_spec.py` and `studio/scene_compose.py` make stickers of two mascots:

```python
from studio.scene_compose import compose_scene
from studio.scene_spec import ActorSpec, SceneSpec
doc, info = compose_scene(SceneSpec(actors=[ActorSpec(base="mochi", motion="wave"), ActorSpec(base="pip", motion="dance")],
                                    caption=Caption("besties")))
```

- A `SceneSpec` holds one or two `ActorSpec`s (the per-mascot fields of a StickerSpec) plus the shared caption, loop
  length, tempo and scene-wide effects. A v3 or v4 StickerSpec loads as a scene of one, writes back in the v4 shape
  and keeps its `key()`; `compose_scene()` sends it through `compose()` unchanged.
- Scene rules (`python -m studio.cli --rules`): S01 at most two mascots, S04 side by side when there is no duet, S06
  two of the same mascot told apart by a fur variant, S07 scene-wide effects drawn around the mascot in front for now,
  S08 the 64 KB budget measured on the built file, S09 one tempo. S02, S03, S05 and S10 arrive with views and duets.
- Command line: `python -m studio.cli "a happy fox waving" --friend dog`, or `--spec scene.json` with an `actors` list.

## Phase 2: the view rig

Two channels turn a mascot away from the viewer, in degrees: `body.yaw` turns the whole mascot (0 faces the viewer,
+ turns the face towards screen-right, 90 is its side, 180 its back) and `head.yaw` turns the head further on top of
it. At 0 nothing new runs, so every file without a turn is byte-identical (379 of 379 snapshot items).

```python
c.set("body.yaw", 45.0)                       # three-quarter, held
M.turn(c, 20, 180.0, dur=30)                  # turn round to face away, the head leading
M.look_back(c, 30, 80, at=-110.0)             # with the body facing away: look back over the shoulder
doc, info = compose(StickerSpec(base="biscuit", yaw=90.0, motion="wave"))     # a sticker seen from the side
```

How a turn is drawn (`mascot/views.py`), per part: the head follows head.yaw + body.yaw, the body body.yaw.

| Turn | Drawn as | Every mascot? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 45 degrees | the front drawing, turned by the rig | yes: nothing to draw |
| 45 to 67.5 | the three-quarter turn held | yes |
| 67.5 to 135 | the drawn side view (mirrored for a negative turn), snapping in with a squash smear | Biscuit; others hold three-quarter |
| 135 to 180 | the drawn back view | Biscuit; others show the side, or the front again |

- **The smooth turn.** Each part is a surface: its outline puffed up into a dome (`view_head` depth 0.85 of the half
  width, plus a snout bump; `view_body` 0.8). Points of the drawing turn with the dome, so eyes, nose and muzzle slide
  towards the side the face turns to; the far eye narrows and the near one widens; the outline only narrows a little.
  Small groups (an eye, the mouth, a shine) move as one piece, bigger ones point by point. Edge rules: a point that
  turns round behind the silhouette rides its edge (stripes and whiskers on the far cheek), and a feature fades as it
  turns away (the far blush). Ears turn like plates standing on the head, the far ear stays behind it.
- **The body.** Shoulders and hip joints ride the body round (the legs are solved from them), feet turn on the ground
  (the far one a little higher), the nearer leg and foot are drawn over the other, the arm on the far side goes behind
  the body past 12 degrees while its paw hangs at the side (a paw raised or on the belly stays in front), and the tail
  swings round behind the body and comes out on the other side past 30 degrees. Things worn on the back turn with it.
- **Drawn views.** `Character.view_data("side" | "back")` returns what differs from the front: drawings (method names),
  anchors in the same coordinates as the front (eyes, mouth, nose, ears, shoulders, hips, feet, tail root, both sides
  given), which way each side's limbs reach (`dirs`), which eyes and cheeks show (`view_sides`) and options (features
  squashed edge-on, no face from behind, tail in front, far arm or both arms behind the body, arms hanging a little
  forward). The rig bakes a copy of the head or the body from that data for each view a clip uses, shows each copy only
  on its frames (hold keys), and drops the front drawing when it is never seen. The side view facing left is the side
  view mirrored, drawn from the channels with left and right swapped, so the paw nearer the viewer is still the one
  that moves. Biscuit's side and back views live in `characters/biscuit.py` (`HEAD_SIDE`, `BODY_SIDE`, `side_*`,
  `back_*`); a mascot built on Biscuit does not inherit them. `problems()` checks the data.
- **Things worn.** Hats show from every side; glasses, masks, aprons, scarves and things worn on the back are drawn
  for the front only, so they are left out while their part is drawn from the side or from behind (each needs a
  drawing per view, later).
- **Studio.** `StickerSpec.yaw` (and `ActorSpec.yaw`), written only when not 0 so every saved spec keeps its key. Three
  motions: `turn_around`, `look_back`, `full_spin` (the old `spin` is unchanged; the parser maps no words to the new ones
  yet, that comes with the phase 3 lexicon). Rule R24 notes a view a mascot has not got (no side view: it turns as far
  as three-quarter; looking back needs a back view, else it looks around).
- **Not yet.** Paws pinned to something (`c.pin`, scene pins) and props placed in world space from a paw are solved
  for the front drawing, so they are only right while the arm is drawn from the front or turned by the rig. The
  three-quarter targets (the drawn outline at 45 degrees the warp blends into) and the bear family's side and back
  views are the rest of phase 2.

Turnaround sheets now show the rig: `python3 tools/turnaround.py biscuit` (front, 20 degrees, three-quarter, side,
back, with the front's guide lines), `python3 tools/turnaround.py --views front,y20,q,side,back` for all 14 (a view a
mascot has not got shows the rig's fallback, faded and marked).
